# 01｜First-season spatial economy

第一季只使用五个空间家族：

| Code | Spatial family | Audience meaning |
|---|---|---|
| A | 主环生活区 | 正常城市 / everyday Chongqing |
| B | 中央共享区 | 跨区公共能力 / shared systems |
| C | 旧模块区（302） | legacy city / old compatibility |
| D | 桥接带 | connection itself becomes drama |
| E | 外缘维护区 | city edge / EVA / detachment |

Outer space is not counted as a city region; it is an exceptional exterior state.

Two recurring bridge assets must become audience-familiar:

1. **Jialing Trunk Bridge** — EP02 forced loss.
2. **Familiar Lifeline Bridge** — EP03 → EP04 → EP08 → EP10.

# 03｜EP01 — normal city → 302 → detached interface → space

## Primary region

> **C｜302 Legacy Module Cluster**

## Supporting regions

- A｜main-ring ordinary city for opening / workday reference；
- E｜outer maintenance edge for the final interface and detachment；
- space for coda.

## Movement

> **A normal city → C 302 public/service side → C/E outer gravity/interface segment → E safe-reset securing point → detached segment → space.**

No traversal through multiple city layers.

## Spatial story

The audience first understands 302 as an old but ordinary connected part of the ring city.

The deeper maintenance move is not a descent through other districts; it is movement from the public face of one module toward its outer service/interface face.

## Critical accident

> **The male is not expelled from the whole 302 district. A small T0-scarred outer interface / maintenance segment loses its structural connection after Plan B has genuinely begun working.**

This is the season's first small-scale “reverse bridge” event.

## State after episode

- 302 remains physically connected and recoverable；
- one outer interface segment is lost/damaged；
- 302 remains an active legacy node for later episodes.

# 04｜EP02 — Jialing Trunk Bridge forced loss

## Primary region

> **D｜Jialing Trunk Bridge**

## Supporting region

> 嘉陵江 shared open corridor / adjacent ring modules.

## Spatial story

This bridge is much larger than the EP01 interface segment and carries city-scale traffic/services.

It has already been supported and repaired for a long time.

The decisive fact is that keeping it connected begins transferring damage into adjacent module interfaces.

## Movement

> rescue/recovery point → bridge verification position → last safe traffic clears → controlled release/retirement.

## End-state

> **Trunk connection exits permanently.**

The gap remains visible in later Jialing views as a city scar.

## Season function

The loss makes lower-capacity routes suddenly matter.

---

# 05｜EP03 — Lifeline proves itself

## Primary region

> **D｜Familiar Lifeline Bridge**

## Supporting regions

- A｜hospital / medical supply origin or destination；
- B/D｜two local interfaces and Gravity Handoff.

## Spatial story

The route is not a secret tunnel.

It is a known low-capacity engineering connection that was never intended to carry ordinary public traffic.

Main bridge loss makes it the only route that can meet the medical waiting window.

## Movement

> hospital/medical staging → A-side bridge interface → central bridge span → B-side receiving interface.

## End-state

The bridge remains physically modest but gains social value.

> **It has saved someone; society now has a reason to ask for it again.**

# 06｜EP04 — temporary connection becomes ordinary life

## Primary regions

> **D Lifeline + C 302 old public layer**

## Main subscene

Old cinema / ordinary public space inside 302 or its immediate legacy-module neighborhood.

## Spatial story

The audience sees the same lifeline functioning with simple schedules, queues and familiar maintenance behavior.

A place that was only a technical fragment becomes reachable enough for one ordinary night of public life.

## Movement

> lifeline crossing → 302 public route → old cinema.

No new megastructure introduced.

## End-state

- lifeline begins becoming infrastructure rather than emergency improvisation；
- 302 starts reading as lived city, not just EP01 accident site.

---

# 07｜EP05 — ordinary private life inside the ring

## Primary region

> **A｜Main Ring Living City**

## Main subscene

Wedding venue / living module cluster near a view into the Jialing corridor or another district.

## Spatial story

This episode deliberately avoids heavy engineering environments.

The audience should feel:

> **people have ordinary homes, ceremonies and social life inside the same city whose connections are breaking.**

Different local public-time states can be visible across the ring / Jialing corridor without requiring a new region.

## End-state

No topology change.

This episode deepens emotional ownership of the city that later contracts.

---

# 08｜EP06 — one shared device, two local times

## Primary region

> **B｜Shared Civic / Utility Belt**

## Main subscene

Shared thermal-storage / environmental-transfer node.

## Spatial relation

The node is physically connected to two local regions through separate interfaces.

Both can directly observe the same device/mechanical phase.

## Movement

> local side A interface → shared equipment space / verification position; local side B remains simultaneously present through its own interface/control side.

## Spatial story

No giant traversal is needed.

The important image is:

> **one physical machine sits between two valid local operating worlds.**

## End-state

One shared action succeeds; no permanent common clock is created.

---

# 09｜EP07 — Shared Hub loses its premise

## Primary region

> **B｜Shared Civic / Utility Belt — Shared Hub subarea**

## Supporting views

A-side and C/legacy-side public consequences.

## Spatial story

The Hub is located in the same shared-system family as EP06, making progression legible:

> EP06 proves one shared physical action can still happen;
> EP07 proves one long-running shared high-order city state can no longer be assumed.

## Movement

Minimal: Hub verification + visible consequences on two connected local regions.

## End-state

> **healthy shared-control function retires.**

The city does not physically break here; its operating topology becomes more local.

---

# 10｜EP08 — same Lifeline, now a legitimacy boundary

## Primary region

> **D｜the exact same Familiar Lifeline Bridge from EP03/04**

## Spatial story

The bridge itself has not become a military border fortress.

It still carries maintenance / medical / essential passage.

What has changed is the public systems on either side.

The same modest industrial bridge now becomes the contact surface between two valid local orders.

## Key fixed geography

- better-resourced side interface；
- degraded/legacy side interface；
- central Gravity Handoff；
- industrial maintenance zone where humanoid robots must remain to keep the lifeline open.

## End-state

The bridge reopens at low capacity, but degraded-side maintenance resources are destroyed/disabled.

The physical connection survives while institutional compatibility becomes more damaged.

---

# 11｜EP09 — return to 302 as a whole place

## Primary region

> **C｜302 Legacy Module Cluster**

## Required visual correction

EP09 must show more than the EP01 Gravity Service Sector.

It should establish that 302 contains:

- people；
- ordinary routes；
- public rooms；
- services；
- working local gravity；
- old but functional interfaces.

## Spatial story

The protagonists return to the same place where EP01 proved current physical reality can veto nominal procedure.

This time they confirm:

> **302 is currently real, safe, occupied and usable.**

The cost is not spatial collapse; it is the permanent compatibility stack required to keep C connected to the future retained A/B/D network.

## End-state

302 remains physically present but enters future transition/migration planning.

---

# 12｜EP10 — final familiar connection

## Primary topology

> **A retained ring city ↔ D Familiar Lifeline ↔ C 302 legacy cluster**

## Supporting state

B/shared functions and other old bridges are already contracting in the background.

## Movement

People move first:

> C 302 → D Lifeline → retained A city.

Then services follow.

Finally the three protagonists remain only long enough to complete the last cutover.

## Sequence

1. residents clear 302；
2. final transit/service clears the familiar lifeline；
3. public energy/data/environment routes retire or transfer；
4. Gravity Handoff unloads；
5. old temporal/protocol compatibility exits；
6. bridge remains structurally healthy；
7. final mechanical connection is released / isolated.

## Spatial payoff

The audience has seen this same physical connection as:

> maintenance bridge → medical lifeline → ordinary route → public-order conflict surface → evacuation route → final healthy connection deliberately ended.

That is the first season's strongest spatial character arc.

# 14｜Scene-design rule for screenplay and visual development

Before adding any new major location, answer:

1. Can this scene happen in A/B/C/D/E instead?
2. Is it actually a new region, or only a room/subscene inside an existing region?
3. Does the audience need to remember this location later?
4. Does adding it improve causality, or only add visual novelty?

Default:

> **reuse familiar spatial assets and change their state.**

First-season visual development should therefore prioritize:

1. ring-city overall orientation image；
2. A main-ring ordinary life；
3. Jialing shared open corridor；
4. C 302 public side；
5. C/E 302 outer service/interface side；
6. D Trunk Bridge；
7. D Familiar Lifeline；
8. B shared thermal/environment node；
9. B Shared Hub；
10. ring-city exterior / EP01 near-space view.

This is enough to give EP01–EP10 a coherent city without designing dozens of independent environments.
